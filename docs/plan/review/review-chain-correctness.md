## CC-01 (major, architecture.md) Reveal-round prediction is fed from the finalized head

- **Location**: §9.3 intro "The scheduler runs per finalized head" (l.2793); step 2 "A single `fetch` (§3.6) at one pinned block reads …" (l.2802); step 7 "`current_block }` // read at ONE block (step 2)" (l.2860); step 10 "the +3-block offset is about 12 rounds of margin" (l.2907); §3.5 `HeadPolicy { …, Finalized /* neuron runtimes */ }` (l.1000).
- **Problem**: the block whose state and number feed `predict_first_reveal_block` and the round formula is left implicit, and the framework defaults make it the finalized head F. The predictor assumes the commit lands at `current_block + 1` and converts `target − current_block` blocks into seconds from wall-clock `now`. With `current_block = F` while the commit lands after the best head B (lag L = B − F): the round is L·block_time too late (L = 3, normal on finney, consumes the entire +3-block `SECURITY_BLOCK_OFFSET` that step 10 budgets as clock-skew margin); the commit epoch is mis-predicted whenever an epoch fires in (F+1, inclusion], so the reveal lands a tempo later and the watcher (which watches the *predicted* epoch) raises a false miss; during a finality stall of about a tempo every commit is encrypted to a round after its reveal epoch, whose queue the pallet then deletes (silent loss, the failure the plan says cannot happen). Python reads all of this at the best head, so this is also an unregistered divergence contradicting step 8's "Reveal rounds therefore differ from Python's only in that edge case".
- **Evidence**: `CORE/timelock/constants.rs:41-44` (`COMMIT_INCLUSION_BLOCK_OFFSET = 1`, "relative to the chain head queried by the SDK"); `CORE/timelock/epoch_schedule.rs:116-117`; `CORE/timelock/mod.rs:172-185`; `PY/intents/weights.py:163, 283-306` (`current_block = await substrate.block_number()`, every schedule item read at that hash) → `PY/_substrate.py:417-418` → `PY/_transport/interface.py:218-226` (`chain_getHeader(None)` = best); `UP/pallets/subtensor/src/coinbase/reveal_commits.rs:46-57` (queues of epochs < reveal epoch removed), `:71-87` (missing pulse retried only inside the reveal epoch); `UP/pallets/drand/src/lib.rs:97` (pulses kept 216,000 rounds, so only late rounds lose).
- **Fix** (drop-in): step 2, append: "The fetch is pinned at the **best** head's hash at the moment the scheduler fires (`HeadPolicy::Best` for this read only; finalized heads still trigger the scheduler and anchor the era): the reveal-round predictor assumes the commit lands in `current_block + 1` (`CORE/timelock/constants.rs:44`), and Python reads the same items at the best head (`PY/intents/weights.py:163, 283-306`). `current_block` in step 7 is that best block's number." Step 12, replace "During the predicted reveal epoch" with "During the commit's actual reveal epoch: after inclusion, read `TimelockedWeightCommits[netuid_index][e]` at the inclusion block for e ∈ {predicted, predicted + 1} and watch `e + RevealPeriodEpochs`; count `predicted ≠ actual` in `rtao_weights_commit_epoch_slip_total`". Step 10: "… about 12 rounds of margin, which assumes `current_block` is the best head (step 2)". Tests: a FakeChain case with finality lag 3 and an epoch due at F+2 asserting the predicted reveal block equals the simulated pallet's; an oracle vector comparing rtao's round with Python's for identical (best block, schedule, `now`) inputs. PLAN.md §7.2: "reads 17 storage items in one RPC at one pinned block" → "… at the best head's hash".

## CC-02 (major, architecture.md) `HyperparamPlanner` rate limits read the wrong storage, and tempo's limit is missing

## CC-03 (major, architecture.md) `retracted` and `finalityTimeout` are not terminal, but the submit path fails on them

- **Location**: §3.9.3 step 4 "`usurped`, `retracted`, `finalityTimeout`, `dropped` and `invalid` are terminal failures, classified through the extracted `_WATCH_STATUS_RE`" (l.1320); §3.9.5/§3.9.6 (only `FateUnknown` is reconciled).
- **Problem**: in the pinned polkadot-sdk, `Retracted` is not final ("Retracted transactions might be included in some next block") and `FinalityTimeout(BlockHash)` means the extrinsic *is* in that block but finality did not arrive within 512 blocks. Under the default `Wait::Finalization`, a short fork makes rtao (like Python) report failure for an extrinsic that then lands, drop the lease and tell CLI users, agents and `OwnerOps` that a transfer or stake failed, which invites a double submission. The framework's "reconciliation is definitive" does not cover it because only `FateUnknown` is reconciled.
- **Evidence**: polkadot-sdk `cacb431` `substrate/client/transaction-pool/api/src/lib.rs:95-125` (doc, item 4; last events), `:144-147` (`Retracted(BlockHash)`, `FinalityTimeout(BlockHash)`), `:162-172` (`is_final` excludes `Retracted`); Python: `PY/_transport/extrinsics.py:352, 367-369` (all five raise).
- **Fix** (replace the bullet): "`usurped` and `invalid` are terminal failures (classified through the extracted `_WATCH_STATUS_RE`). `retracted` is not terminal: keep watching for a later `inBlock`/`finalized`; if the subscription ends first, reconcile (§3.9.6) until the finalized head passes `birth + period`. `finalityTimeout(h)`: resolve the outcome from block `h` (§3.9.4) and confirm finality by following finalized heads. `dropped`: with `reconcile` on, treat as `FateUnknown` and reconcile; with it off, keep Python's failure. The lease settles only when an outcome is resolved." Register D-024 (`improved`; Python reports failure for a retracted extrinsic that later lands; test `retracted_then_finalized_is_success`). Add `FakeNode` scripts `ready → inBlock(A) → retracted(A) → inBlock(B) → finalized(B)` (success at B, one settled lease) and `finalityTimeout(A)` (receipt of A).

## CC-04 (minor, architecture.md) Multisig: the threshold-reaching approval must be `as_multi` with the call

- **Location**: §3.12 "the opening round uses `as_multi` with the full call; later rounds use `approve_as_multi` with the hash;" (l.1471).
- **Problem**: `approve_as_multi` that reaches the threshold only records `MultisigApproval`; `pallet_multisig` dispatches only when the call bytes are supplied. Implemented as written (including `compose::as_multi`), the final approval never executes the operation while reporting success. Python keeps hash-only approvals intermediate (`multisig_approve`) and executes with `as_multi` plus the full call (`multisig_execute`, and the account-object path).
- **Evidence**: polkadot-sdk `cacb431` `substrate/frame/multisig/src/lib.rs:442` ("If this is the final approval, you will want to use `as_multi` instead"), `:671` (dispatch only when the call is present and approvals ≥ threshold), `:710` (otherwise `MultisigApproval`); `PY/intents/multisig.py:403-416` ("It never executes — … the last signatory must send `multisig_execute` with the full call"), `:440-463`; `PY/multisig.py:249-275`.
- **Fix**: "the opening approval uses `as_multi` with the full call (`maybe_timepoint = None`); intermediate approvals use `approve_as_multi` with the call hash and the opening timepoint (`multisig_approve`); the approval that reaches the threshold uses `as_multi` with the full call and the timepoint (`multisig_execute`, and always on the saved-multisig adapter path), because `pallet_multisig` dispatches only when the call is supplied (`frame/multisig/src/lib.rs:671`). `compose::as_multi` takes `CallOrHash` and refuses `Hash` when `Multisig.Multisigs[account, call_hash].approvals + 1 >= threshold`."

## CC-05 (minor, architecture.md) The shield nonce pair needs a stated cache postcondition; Python's sequence reuses n+1

- **Location**: §3.11 step 2 "`nonce_manager.lease_pair(signer)` returns n (carrier) and n+1 (inner)." (l.1425); §3.9.1 "Nonces follow Python" and `pin` "explicit nonce (Python `pin`)".
- **Problem**: Python takes n from the cache, signs the inner at n+1 (`pin(n+1)`), then the carrier at n (`pin(n)`), so after a successful shielded submission the cache holds n and the next submission from that process gets n+1, the inner's nonce, which the pool rejects as `Stale` once the inner lands. A file-for-file port of `_submit_encrypted_call` through `pin` reproduces it. The plan never states what `lease_pair` leaves in the cache.
- **Evidence**: `PY/executor.py:1037-1049`; `PY/_transport/interface.py:519-523` (explicit nonce → `pin`); `PY/_transport/extrinsics.py:67-81` (`next_for` increments, `pin` overwrites); `UP/runtime/src/check_nonce.rs:145-146` (`Stale` when nonce < account nonce).
- **Fix**: append to step 2: "`lease_pair` reserves n and n+1 under one fetch lock and leaves `next = n + 2`; the carrier lease settles on carrier inclusion and the inner lease on inner inclusion; an unsettled inner lease clears the cache. rtao never calls `pin(n)` after `pin(n+1)`. Python leaves its cache at n (`PY/executor.py:1037-1049`, `PY/_transport/extrinsics.py:79-81`), so its next submission reuses n+1: register D-026 (`improved`), test `shielded_then_plain_uses_n_plus_2`."

## CC-06 (minor, architecture.md) Shield eras anchored at the finalized head fail with certainty under finality lag; `P4` is a trap

- **Location**: §3.9.2 "If finality lags by more than `p/2`, the submission still proceeds …" (l.1295-1297); §3.11 `pub enum ShieldPeriod { P4, P8 }` (l.1417); decision 15.
- **Problem**: carrier and inner are signed with a period-4 or period-8 era anchored at F. The pool validates at B+1, and the extrinsic is valid only while `birth(current) == F`, that is for blocks below F + period. With `P8` and a lag of 7 or more (with `P4`, already at a lag of 3, which is normal on finney) the carrier is rejected (`BadProof` or `AncientBirthBlock`) on every attempt, and the plan says the submission "still proceeds". `burned_register` and `add_collateral` are always shielded.
- **Evidence**: `UP/runtime/src/check_mortality.rs:91-111`; polkadot-sdk `cacb431` `substrate/frame/system/src/extensions/check_mortality.rs:64-69, 98-101`, `substrate/primitives/runtime/src/generic/era.rs:86-99`; `CORE/codec/extrinsic.rs:407-412`; `PY/_transport/interface.py:485-499`.
- **Fix**: add to §3.11: "Before leasing the pair, read the best head B and the finalized head F. If `B + 1 >= F + period`, refuse with `TxError::ShieldEraExpired { period, finality_lag: B - F }` (the carrier would fail `CheckMortality` as `BadProof`/`AncientBirthBlock`); warn when `B − F > period / 2`. `P4` is accepted only when `B − F ≤ 1`, and the CLI never defaults to it. Registered as D-025 (`stricter`); FakeChain tests at lags 1, 4 and 7 for `P8` and 2 and 3 for `P4`." In §3.9.2 add "(shield periods: §3.11 refusal rule)".

## CC-07 (minor, architecture.md) D-011's premise is false: Python never retries shielded submissions

- **Location**: §3.9.6 "`Stale` on a shield carrier is never retried (D-011). Python's substring match retries it …, but for a carrier it means the era was wrong, and `ShieldPeriod` … makes that unrepresentable anyway" (l.1376-1379); Appendix C D-011 (l.4037); Appendix D item 5 (l.4079); PLAN.md §8.4 D-011 and §15 Python-side issues.

- **Evidence**: `PY/executor.py:812-824` (redirect) vs `:833-845` (retry loop), `:897-909` (signature); `PY/cli/context.py:1491-1502`; `UP/runtime/src/check_nonce.rs:145-146`; `UP/runtime/src/check_mortality.rs:94-100`.

With `ShieldPeriod` the era `Stale` is unrepresentable, so a carrier `Stale` is `CheckNonce`'s outdated nonce (`check_nonce.rs:145-146`): it is surfaced as `PoolRejection::Stale`, both pair leases drop, and the caller may resubmit." Delete D-011 from Appendix C and PLAN.md §8.4, and drop the matching Python-side issue (Appendix D item 5, PLAN.md §15).

## CC-08 (minor, architecture.md) Reveal-time failures are silent and unattributed; a Null subnet without a permit is a certain rejection

- **Location**: §9.3 step 2 "**Warnings only**: missing validator permit and stake below `StakeThreshold`" (l.2813); step 12 reveal watcher (l.2918-2920).
- **Problem**: (a) on Null subnets a missing permit is rejected at the pool and at dispatch, so by the plan's own rule it is a refusal, not a warning. (b) The pallet reveals by calling `do_set_mechanism_weights`, which re-runs the min-stake, version-key, permit, uid-validity and length checks at the reveal block. - **Evidence**: `UP/pallets/subtensor/src/guards/check_weights.rs:97-112` (Null permit and min stake at the pool; `NeuronNoValidatorPermit` maps to custom 255, `extensions/subtensor.rs:36-70`); `subnets/weights.rs:17-26` (`ensure_null_weight_permit` at commit dispatch), `:1015, 1021, 1037, 1046, 1052` (checks re-run at reveal), `:1350-1369` (Yuma self/owner exceptions); `coinbase/reveal_commits.rs:188-202` (`Err` → `log::trace`, `continue`, no event).
- **Fix**: step 2, add a refusal: "`SubnetEpochConsensus == Null` and `ValidatorPermit[netuid][uid] == false` (certain pool rejection, `guards/check_weights.rs:97-104`)". Step 12, append: "The pallet reveals through `do_set_mechanism_weights` and drops failures silently (`reveal_commits.rs:188-202`). On a miss the watcher reads, at the first block of the reveal epoch, `ValidatorPermit[uid]`, the hotkey's stake against `StakeThreshold`, `WeightsVersionKey`, `SubnetworkN`, `MinAllowedWeights` and `Uids[netuid][hotkey]`, and labels `rtao_weights_reveal_missed_total{mech, reason}` with the first failing check, or `drand_round` when `Drand.LastStoredRound < round`.
