## Wire summaries

### rtao-compat-synapse (M10-01)

Wire compatibility with `bittensor==10.5.0`. A v10 Dendrite posts a
JSON-encoded pydantic model over plain HTTP and signs
`"{nonce}.{hotkey}.{axon_hotkey}.{uuid}.{body_hash}"` with sr25519, carrying
signature and metadata in `bt_header_*` headers. The body hash is Python's
`str()`/`repr()` of the subnet's `required_hash_fields` (`PyValue`,
`py_repr`, `get_hash`); the rest of the body is unauthenticated, as in
Python. The crate provides the codec, Python's `default_verify` with stricter
defaults, an axum Axon (feature `axon`), a reqwest Dendrite (feature
`dendrite`), streaming responses, and the msgpack-numpy tensor form (feature
`tensor`).

Divergences, all compatible with honest 10.5.0 peers:

- the nonce window always applies (the unsigned `bt_header_dendrite_version`
  cannot disable it); nonces more than 60 s ahead are refused; non-finite or
  negative timeouts are refused and timeout widening is capped at 300 s;
- the nonce table is bounded, expires entries once no replay could pass,
  answers 503 when full and limits live `hotkey:uuid` keys per hotkey;
- header prefixes match only at position zero, duplicate signed headers are
  refused and `bt_header_input_obj_*` headers are ignored;
- handlers receive the verified header identity, and the body's
  `dendrite`/`axon` objects are replaced before forwarding;
- the Axon does not sign attacker-chosen text before verification
  (`AxonSignature::Omit` by default) and the default verification always runs;
- request bodies are limited (16 MiB by default, 413 above).

`V10VerifyPolicy::python` restores Python's verification behaviour for
differential tests (it then needs `allow_unbounded_nonce_table`).

`rtao-btauth`'s `legacy` module gains `verify_v10_for` and a strict
btwallet-compatible signature decoder, with three tests.

### rtao-compat-epistula (M10-02)

Epistula v2 signs `sha256(body).hexdigest() "." uuid "." timestamp_ms "."
receiver` with the sender's sr25519 hotkey in `Epistula-*` headers. Upstream
implementations disagree on details, so dialect presets RTAO, SPEC, BITMIND,
GOBT and APEX select the exact message, receiver, timestamp and
secret-signature rules. Replay protection uses `rtao_btauth::NonceStore`
(in-memory or Redis). The crate provides `EpistulaLayer` and `DualAuthLayer`
(Epistula or btauth/1 on one route), a secret-signature filter and a client
helper. Signing and verification are pure until a verified request is
committed to the replay store.

### rtao-transport-quic (M10-03)

A reimplementation of the btlightning wire protocol (pinned at `53c090b`,
v0.2.15) on quinn 0.11 and rustls with the ring provider only. It does not
depend on btlightning or its `sp-core` 34, so it uses rtao's own signing
stack. MessagePack maps use positive-integer array encoding as btlightning
does; `data::from_data` accepts every enum shape btlightning encoders emit.

Differences, invisible on the wire:

- the client verifies the server's TLS 1.3 `CertificateVerify` signature
  (btlightning's own client does not; noted here and not filed upstream under
  the no-new-filings rule);
- the server authorizes per QUIC connection, not per remote address, and
  allows one validator identity per connection;
- replay nonces live for the signature age plus future skew (plus a clock-step
  margin) and the store fails closed at capacity;
- unauthenticated connections are capped, evicted oldest-first from the
  busiest source, limited to small frames and closed after the handshake
  timeout; per-source limits group IPv6 by /64;
- reply writes have a deadline, and a streaming reply is never cut inside a
  frame.

### rtao-remote-signer (M10-04)

Protocol `rtao-remote-signer/1`, an rtao addition with no Python counterpart:

- `POST /sign` carries the `SigningRequest` minus secrets; `GET /accounts`
  lists signable accounts. Byte strings are strict lowercase `0x` hex,
  accounts are SS58 (emitted at prefix 42, accepted at any valid prefix and
  compared as bytes), and objects reject unknown fields.
- Requests are authenticated with btauth/1 using a client key separate from
  the signing account. Responses are self-authenticating: the client checks
  the returned signature against its own payload.
- The reference server decodes the call strictly against its own trusted
  runtime, re-derives every signed byte, applies `rtao_tx::Policy` and
  account/call allowlists, and only then signs. `rtao-remote-signerd` is the
  daemon binary (feature `server-bin`).
- Replay and tamper refusal are covered by tests.

## Plan deviation

PLAN placed `RemoteSigner` in `rtao-signer` behind feature `remote`.
`rtao-btauth` depends on `rtao-signer`, and `RemoteSigner` needs btauth/1, so
that placement would form a dependency cycle. `RemoteSigner` is therefore the
separate crate `rtao-remote-signer`, exposed as `rtao::remote_signer`. PLAN
§4.2, §4.3, §4.4, D-031 (§8 and `parity/divergences.toml`), §12.12 and §16
are updated accordingly.
